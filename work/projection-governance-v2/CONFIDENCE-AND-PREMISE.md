# Standing confidence and premise rules — effective 2026-09-19

Every newly written or revised report ends with a worded confidence rating on its central claim, concerning survival under independent recomputation on authoritative data, not successful execution. State the applicable definition and what would move the rating down one level. Numeric intervals remain separate in tables.

- low: the claim rests on one series or one season and could invert.
- medium: it holds on authoritative data but depends on a choice that could reasonably differ.
- high: it holds across seasons and survives the obvious alternative specifications.
- near-total: it is arithmetic on verified rows.

Required ending: Confidence: <rating> — <applicable definition>. Move down one level if <specific evidence or choice changes>. For low, state what would invalidate the claim rather than inventing a fifth rating.

Before any experiment fitting, publish one premise line identifying the control series, whether it is the authoritative deployed lineage, and its generation date. A non-authoritative control blocks the experiment. Validate its byte hash and authority against work/series-registry/catalog.json; reports never infer authority from similar numerical results. E-POST's premise error is a standing example.

No candidate fitting in E-QB-CHANGE until its diagnostic distinguishes starter identification from quarterback valuation. Unknown historical knowledge is not classified as not knowable.
