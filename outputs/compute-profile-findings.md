# NFL engine efficiency findings

Private research update, September 6, 2026. The public release remains the front-page Beta presentation.

The completed synthetic profile redirects the next optimization toward probability calculations and probability-array serialization. Grid insertion is too small a target to pursue alone. This is an engineering finding; no faster implementation or predictive improvement has been established.

The successful attempt took **2.75 seconds and 135.34 MiB**. Eight cases covered three-atom controls and 700-atom synthetic supports, with 64 paired measurements of the complete original and instrumented fitting/scoring callbacks. All cases used 64×64 primary and 128×128 doubled grids, with full scoring diagnostics. The instrumented forecasts and metrics matched the original canonical bytes, and the recorded grid hashes matched the captured original grids. All 66 frozen source files remained unchanged.

On the 700-atom workload, summing separate fitting and scoring maxima gives an optimistic grid-insertion removal estimate of about **6.2%** for callbacks matching the pilot's roles, or **3.6%** across the broader stress cases. Even the corresponding whole-grid estimates are only **10.3%** and **6.3%**. These are diagnostic estimates from synthetic timings, with measurement noise. They do not bound historical runtime. They are far below the **52.47%** combined fit/scorer reduction needed to pass the stopped backtest's unchanged equation with other terms fixed.

The additional function profile attributed about **71–86% of scoring time** to cumulative probability evaluation, including the probability queries used to find interval endpoints. Probability-array JSON encoding was also substantial inside the complete fitting, recovery and authentication callback. These cumulative timings overlap their child functions and include profiling overhead; they are useful for locating work, not predicting an achieved speedup.

The subsequent [candidate tests](compute-candidate-findings.md) examined a guarded Poisson-CDF helper and an exact encoder for flat probability arrays, separately and together. They preserved outputs but fell short of the complete-callback cost target. Both must preserve probability values, serialized bytes, failures, forecast recovery and all diagnostics. Any claimed efficiency gain must include complete callback costs and preparation. The pinned SciPy implementation delegates its Poisson CDF to [the documented `pdtr` function](https://docs.scipy.org/doc/scipy-1.13.1/reference/generated/scipy.special.pdtr.html); equivalence still needs to be tested for our actual input domain. No new historical run is underway.

Two failed profiling attempts remain saved: one exposed a callback argument-name collision, and one exposed a native-array serialization issue. Their unsaved calculations provide no timing evidence. The successful profile includes independent implementation and numerical review. Those reviews checked retained hashes and arithmetic without regenerating grids or metrics; raw grid arrays were not retained. The original tiny fixture actually has 6/4 atoms in its two availability windows; the new three-atom control is a separate construction.

RF-02F remains closed as `protocol_invalid` at its cost checkpoint. Split-rate predictive performance, the external 5% target and prospective confirmation remain unproven.
