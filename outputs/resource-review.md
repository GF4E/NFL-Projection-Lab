## What testing taught us

WinWithOdds' [homepage](https://www.winwithodds.com/) reports PPR correlations of 0.544 for its projections and 0.532 for FantasyPros. Those are site-reported summaries, not an independently reproduced comparison. The explanatory text says 2026 while the table labels the summary 2025. The reviewed public pages did not establish an immutable historical forecast archive or joint home/away team-score distribution.

The [methodology example](https://www.winwithodds.com/about) also has inconsistent American-to-decimal conversions. By arithmetic, −120 corresponds to 1.8333 and +340 to 4.4; the example shows 2 and 5.25. This is a documentation finding, not proof that the running implementation uses those values. Any future probability conversion or de-vigging implementation should be verified independently before use.

Our decision is to retain useful source leads and testable ideas without treating advertised accuracy as model evidence. Player fantasy correlation, point forecasts and team ratings do not by themselves support the goal's joint team-score energy comparison. Odds-derived projections belong in the separately governed market comparison stage; they do not become inputs to the football-only model.

## Efficient next step

Inspect one permitted historical forecast example per plausible comparator before spending time on a bulk archive. It needs a stable game ID, original issue time, model/version, target definition, forecast values and revision history. For the primary 5% comparison, require a joint score distribution or samples, or a separately frozen adapter with its assumptions explicitly evaluated. Then check full population coverage and matched forecast origins.

Historical exports Gabe already has, with their original issue dates, would help most. Service names and available years are enough to determine the next small check. These leads do not change the external 5% threshold, authorize a subscription, or establish a qualified comparator.
